# Theory and references

Personalysis draws on personality psychology, personality-judgment research, motivation, appraisal, and interpersonal theory to interpret behavior in context. Use these perspectives as internal analytical lenses to read subtext, context, and dynamics—not as academic disclaimers to recite to the reader. Use this reference to look up the original papers, the concepts they introduce, and how Personalysis applies them.

## Eight perspectives

| Perspective and original source | Definition | Personalysis application | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole Trait Theory — [Fleeson and Jayawickreme, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4472377/); [Fleeson, 2001](https://personality-project.org/revelle/syllabi/classreadings/fleeson.2001.pdf) | Traits have a descriptive distribution of momentary states and explanatory social-cognitive processes. | Compare conditions: someone covers urgent work but declines weekend requests. Preserve both observations before generalizing. | A single conversation captures situated tendencies within that relationship rather than a complete life sample; calibrate confidence to the material's coverage. |
| Realistic Accuracy Model (RAM) — [Funder, 1995](https://rap.ucr.edu/FunderPR1995.pdf) | Accurate judgment requires relevant cues to be available, detected, and appropriately used. | Link a claim to its source and context; weigh concrete cues and subtext together before stating the main reading. | Anchor readings in concrete cues rather than over-reading silence; agreement among models does not replace cue quality. |
| Self–Other Knowledge Asymmetry (SOKA) — [Vazire, 2010](https://www.simine.com/docs/Vazire_JPSP_2010.pdf) | Self–other judgment asymmetries involve both access to information and motivational biases, depending on observability and evaluativeness. | Separate calm wording from the speaker's later report of anxiety; revise the interpretation when that information arrives. | Neither the person nor the observer is always right; treat differing viewpoints as distinct vantage points rather than automatic proof that one side lacks self-knowledge. |
| Traits, adaptations, and narrative identity — [McAdams and Pals, 2006](https://www.e-stimate.dk/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/McAdams-Fundamental-Principles-for-an-Integrative-Science-of-Personality.pdf); [McAdams and McLean, 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/0963721413475622/) | Dispositions, contextual goals and adaptations, and personally constructed life stories address different aspects of a person. | Distinguish “I often help,” “I want this partnership to work,” and “I used to define myself through being needed.” | Evidence categories guide internal analysis rather than becoming academic labels in prose. Let childhood origins and life narratives come from the person, not invention. |
| Self-Determination Theory (SDT) — [Ryan and Deci, 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf) | Autonomy, competence, and relatedness concern volition, effectiveness, and connection. | If someone welcomes responsibility but dislikes prescribed steps, examine limits on autonomy and what choice means to them. | Autonomy is not independence, and wanting choice is not wanting distance; needs are not personality types. |
| Cognitive appraisal and coping — [Folkman et al., 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712234/); [Lazarus, 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1928936/) | Emotions and coping relate to an event's personal significance and perceived coping resources. | An unanswered message may initially mean rejection to its sender; learning about illness can shift that reading. | Separate how deeply something hurt from what the other person necessarily intended or did. |
| Interpersonal circumplex and complementarity — [Sadler et al., 2009](https://uwaterloo.ca/psychology/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/Sadler_et_al_2009_JPSP_Are_We_On_the_Same_Wavelength.pdf) | Interactions vary along dominance–submission and warmth–hostility; partners' responses can coordinate probabilistically. | Examine successive proposals and concessions from both speakers, including how roles shift or escalate over time. | Notice how both sides shape a loop without equating complementarity with relationship health or assigning unilateral blame. |
| Person-centered approach — [Rogers, 1957](https://larrygmaguire.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/The-Necessary-and-Sufficient-Conditions-of-Therapeutic-Personality-Change.pdf) | Empathic understanding, congruence, and positive regard are among proposed conditions in human therapeutic relationships. | When someone expresses envy and shame, acknowledge their experience warmly and leave room for correction and choice. | Empathize with feelings honestly without endorsing harmful acts or unverified claims; generated empathy is not clinical treatment. |

## Optional personality frameworks

The Myers–Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is optional, not required for Personalysis. In chat or text analysis, MBTI describes **behavioral tendencies expressed within that material or relationship** using clear confidence tiers (**clear tendency** when repeated across episodes, **mild lean** when cues are gentler or narrower, and **insufficient cues** only when relevant signals are genuinely absent)—distinct from a lifelong clinical verdict or standardized questionnaire score. Treat a supplied questionnaire type as an attributed report. Big Five dimensions describe openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism.

The frameworks can be related. [McCrae and Costa, 1989](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1458465/) found that the four MBTI indices measured aspects of four Big Five dimensions. The same study did not support truly dichotomous preferences or qualitatively distinct personality types. These findings permit evidence-based comparisons without establishing a mechanical conversion rule from an MBTI type to an individual's Big Five test scores. State which measures and evidence support a comparison rather than treating shared terminology as equivalence.

**Fictional example:** A person reports an introverted MBTI preference and a middle-range Big Five extraversion result, then asks whether they conflict. Discuss the relationship between the constructs while keeping the two reported results and their measurement contexts distinct. The results need not be contradictory, and neither result alone establishes how the person behaves in every setting. Frequent work presentations can prompt a question about role demands and enjoyment, but do not settle either result. A useful answer explains the possible overlap without inventing a replacement score or dismissing either report.
